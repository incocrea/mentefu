Dojo · Yellow belt

Level 2 — Awareness

Belt yellow

GuiltFuDojoLearnDetectTrainDefendStories

The idea of this level

In the previous level you learned to see guilt when it shows up. Now comes the second move: the center. In martial arts, keeping your center means not losing your balance when something pushes you. Here it means something similar: when guilt pushes you to apologize, give in or do what you did not want to do, don’t move yet. Pause.

That pause is not passivity. It is the space where everything interesting happens. Because guilt never arrives alone: it arrives with a voice. A voice that accuses, that passes sentence, that says “how selfish”, “what were you thinking”, “after everything they did for you”. Norberto Levy called that voice the inner accuser, and the idea of this level is simple: before you obey it, listen to it. Carefully. The way you would listen to someone for the first time.

Because when you really listen, something curious happens. The voice has a tone, a vocabulary, a way of scolding… that is not always yours.

Missions in this level

Each mission is a few minutes of cards, questions and micro-challenges. Complete them in order and finish with the belt exam.

Your progress

Supporting scrolls

  1. The inner accuser — who is talking inside your head when you feel guilty.
  2. Pause Fu — the tool for not answering right away. At this level you only need the first two letters: pause and analyze.

The exercise

Keep writing things down as you did in level 1, but add one step. Every time guilt appears, before you do anything:

  1. Pause. Count to five, breathe, step out of the room for a moment, whatever works. All you need is to not react in the first second.
  2. Write down literally what the voice says. Not a summary: the exact words, with their adjectives and their tone. “You only ever think about yourself.” “Here you go again.” “What will people say.”
  3. Ask yourself who it sounds like. Whose tone is that? Who used those words? Your mother, a teacher, an ex, a boss, a version of you from fifteen years ago? Sometimes it is a mix. Sometimes it is someone who is no longer in your life and still hands out opinions for free.

Do this for one week. Do not try to argue with the voice or silence it; that is training for later levels. Just transcribe and sign: who said what.

By the end you will have a small collection of phrases and, almost certainly, a pattern: two or three voices that keep coming back. That is your inner accuser, with a first and last name. And discovering that it speaks with an accent that is not yours already changes the relationship: obeying your conscience is one thing; obeying an echo is something else entirely.

One important clarification: the fact that the voice sounds like someone does not mean that person hurt you on purpose, nor that everything it says is false. Sometimes the inner accuser is right. For now it is not your job to judge it; only to identify it.

The question to take with you

This level’s question

Who is talking inside my head when I feel guilty?

When to move to the next level

When, most of the time, you manage to fit a pause between guilt and reaction — even a three-second one — and you can recognize the voice: “ah, this is my father”, “this is school”, “this one really is me”. You do not need to know yet whether the accusation is fair; that is the question of level 3. Mark the level when it feels that way — not when you finish reading.
