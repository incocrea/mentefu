Level 3 — Analysis

Belt orange

GuiltFuDojoLearnDetectTrainDefendStories

The idea of this level

You can already see guilt, and you already know who is talking. Now comes the part that looks most like a fight, even though it isn’t one: the dodge. When the inner accuser throws an accusation, you do not have to take it head-on. You can let it pass while you look at it. “Don’t accept the accusation automatically” does not mean “reject every accusation”; it means that between the blow and the “yes, I’m guilty” there is a space in which to examine.

Examine what? The rule. Every guilt carries a rule behind it: you feel guilty because you believe you should have done something different. And every “should” has a history: someone said it, someone repeated it, you learned it, and at some point it lost its signature. It became law. This level is also the disarm: taking the rule, turning it over and reading the label. Who made it? Does it still serve?

Careful, though: this is not about throwing out every rule. Many of the ones you inherited are good, and you would keep them anyway if you were given the choice. That is exactly what the exercise is about: for once, being given the choice.

Missions in this level

Each mission is a few minutes of cards, questions and micro-challenges. Complete them in order and finish with the belt exam.

The exercise

Make a list of your ten most frequent “shoulds”. Not the most serious ones: the most frequent, the ones that play every day. If in doubt, go back to your notes from levels 1 and 2; almost all of them are in there. Typical examples: “I should reply right away”, “I should visit my parents more”, “I should make better use of my time”, “I should be more patient”, “I shouldn’t rest while other people are working”.

For each one, three columns:

  1. The “should”, written exactly as it sounds.
  2. Whose it is. Where you learned it: family, school, religion, a partner, work, social media, “people”. If you don’t know, write “I don’t know”; that is already a data point.
  3. Does it still represent my values? Yes, no, or “partly”. And if it is a “yes”, ask yourself why. If the answer is “because otherwise I’d feel terrible”, that is not a value, that is fear of guilt. If the answer is “because I care”, then that rule is yours, and it is welcome to stay.

Take your time, over two or three sittings. What usually happens is that several of the ten turn out to belong to someone else and no longer represent you, others really are yours and you are glad to have them, and two or three sit in a grey zone that you will keep chewing on for a while. That is fine. The question “what happens if I don’t?” tends to clear up the grey zones quite a bit: very often the honest answer is “nothing, except that I feel guilty”.

The question to take with you

This level’s question

Who decided that I should? And do I still agree?

When to move to the next level

When a “should” shows up and asking whose it is comes almost on its own, before you obey it. And when you have at least one inherited rule you have decided to keep out of conviction, and another you have decided to stop following, even if it still costs you. You do not need the whole list resolved; grey zones can be carried around for a while. Mark the level when it feels that way — not when you finish reading.
