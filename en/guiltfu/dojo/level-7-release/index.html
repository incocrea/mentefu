Dojo · Brown belt

Level 7 — Release

Belt brown

GuiltFuDojoLearnDetectTrainDefendStories

The idea of this level

There is a guilt that does not leave even after you repair. You already apologized, you already fixed what could be fixed, you already learned the lesson, and still, at three in the morning, it comes back: “I should have…”, “if only I had…”, “why did I…”. That is not moral conscience; it is rumination. And rumination has one feature that gives it away: it produces nothing. It does not repair, it does not learn, it does not decide. It only repeats.

The last combat principle is release: stop punishing yourself once the repair has been made. Notice the condition: once it has been made. This is not a general amnesty; it is justice. In any reasonable system, a fault carries a proportionate penalty and, once the penalty is served, the matter is closed. Your inner accuser, on the other hand, tends to work like a court that hands down a sentence, carries it out, and the next day tries the same case all over again. For years. This level is about closing the file.

It is not easy, which is why it is the brown belt. Learning from the past does not mean living in it, but punishing yourself gives you a strange feeling of doing something: as if suffering over a mistake were a way of honoring it. It is not. What honors a mistake is repairing it and not repeating it; the rest is noise that, on top of everything, drains the energy you need for what actually matters.

Missions in this level

Each mission is a few minutes of cards, questions and micro-challenges. Complete them in order and finish with the belt exam.

The exercise

Choose an old mistake that keeps coming back. One you have already repaired, or one that can no longer be repaired because time has passed, the person is gone or the situation no longer exists. Write its sentence served, in three parts, like an official document:

  1. What happened. Facts, no adjectives. What you did or failed to do, and who it affected.
  2. What you repaired. What you did afterwards: the apology, the gesture, the change. If you could not repair anything because it was no longer possible, write that down too; it is part of the sentence.
  3. What you learned. What you now do differently because of — or thanks to — that.

Underneath, one more line: the closing line. Short, yours, to say when the rumination comes back. Something like “this has already been judged”, “I already paid for this”, “I repaired it, I learned from it, I move on”. It does not have to sound pretty; it has to sound final. The rumination will return, because it is a habit, and habits do not leave by decree. But each time it returns you will have a document to read instead of a trial to repeat.

If, while writing this, you notice that the self-reproach is very intense, that it has been going on for a long time, or that it spreads to almost everything you do, this is where what this Dojo can offer you ends and what a mental health professional can offer you begins. Asking for that help is not a failure of the training; it is the right move.

The question to take with you

This level’s question

If I have already repaired it, what am I still punishing myself for?

When to move to the next level

When you have at least one sentence served in writing and have actually used the closing line, on one of those nights, and confirmed that it helps, even a little. When the rumination still shows up but no longer drags you under: you see it, you name it, you read it the file. Mark the level when it feels that way — not when you finish reading.
