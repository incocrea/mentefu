Dojo · Blue belt

Level 5 — Autonomy

Belt blue

GuiltFuDojoLearnDetectTrainDefendStories

The idea of this level

Blue belt: boundaries. Until now you have recognized, listened, questioned and blocked. All of that happens on the inside. This is the first level where something changes on the outside: you are going to say no.

First, a distinction that is worth the whole level. Faced with any request there are at least four ways of responding that look very similar from the outside and have nothing in common on the inside:

  • Deciding: you look at what you want, what matters to you and what it costs, and you choose. The result can be a yes or a no.
  • Obeying: you do what is expected because it is expected. You don’t even look.
  • Pleasing: you do what the other person wants so they won’t be upset, so they will keep liking you, so there is no scene.
  • Sacrificing yourself: you do what the other person wants at your own expense — and you keep the receipt.

Saying yes is not the problem. Saying yes without having decided is. And many people have been in pleasing mode for so long that they cannot remember what a decision of their own sounds like. The move that helps here is the deflect: when the thought says “if I say no, I’m a bad person”, you reword it into something more accurate: “if I say no, someone will be upset for a while and I will feel guilty for a while. Both of those pass.”

Missions in this level

Each mission is a few minutes of cards, questions and micro-challenges. Complete them in order and finish with the belt exam.

The exercise

This week, say a small “no”. Genuinely small: do not break up with anyone, do not quit anything. A plan you don’t feel like, a favor that doesn’t suit you, a “can you take a quick look at this?” when you don’t have a quick moment. Choose something whose worst possible outcome you could easily survive.

Three rules:

  1. Don’t over-explain. One reason is enough; five is an apology. “I can’t this week” is a complete sentence. If you notice you are still talking, stop.
  2. Before you say it, write down what you fear will happen. Literally: “they’ll get angry”, “they’ll think I’m selfish”, “they’ll never ask me for anything again”. With the date and time.
  3. Afterwards, write down what actually happened. With the same honesty. Then compare the two notes.

Most often, the gap between what you feared and what happened is enormous: the other person said “oh, okay” and got on with their life. Sometimes they do get upset. Then it is time to look at whether that upset is yours to manage or theirs to get over, and it is almost always the latter. And if the “no” goes completely wrong, that is information too: it says something about that relationship, not about your right to refuse.

If there is guilt afterwards — there will be — don’t try to make it disappear. Let it be there, look at it the way you did in level 1, and check just one thing: is anyone harmed, or is someone simply displeased? They are not the same.

The question to take with you

This level’s question

This thing I’m about to do — am I deciding it, obeying it, pleasing someone, or sacrificing myself?

When to move to the next level

When you have said at least one “no” without over-explaining, survived the guilt that followed, and confirmed that reality was gentler than your forecast. It does not need to feel natural yet; it is enough to have seen that it can be done. Mark the level when it feels that way — not when you finish reading.
