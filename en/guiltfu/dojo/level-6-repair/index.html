Level 6 — Repair

Belt purple

GuiltFuDojoLearnDetectTrainDefendStories

The idea of this level

This is the level that keeps this whole school from turning into an excuse. Up to here you have learned not to obey guilt automatically. But sometimes guilt is right. Sometimes you did cause harm. And a trained person is not someone who never feels guilty: it is someone who can tell when guilt is pointing at something real and, in that case, acts.

That is the principle of repair: take responsibility when it is yours to take. When it is yours — no more, no less. The interesting thing is that functional guilt — the kind that recognizes a mistake, points to a harm and pushes you to fix it — is brief and useful. It does its job and leaves. Dysfunctional guilt, on the other hand, does not want you to repair: it wants you to suffer. It turns a mistake into an identity (“I’m a disaster”), hangs around for months and, curiously, tends to get in the way of repairing, because you are so busy punishing yourself that there is no energy left to pick up the phone and apologize.

Separating responsibility from guilt is this: responsibility is what you can do; guilt is what you feel. You can have a lot of the second and none of the first (you did no harm, someone simply got upset), or a fair amount of the first and little of the second (you messed up, you fix it, you move on). The training consists of looking at the two separately.

Missions in this level

Each mission is a few minutes of cards, questions and micro-challenges. Complete them in order and finish with the belt exam.

The exercise

Choose a real guilt. Not one of those you have already dismantled in earlier levels: one where you suspect there is genuinely something of yours in it. A harsh reply, something you forgot that hurt someone, a promise you did not keep, a comment that is still going round in your head.

Run it through the full worksheet: what happened, which part depends on you, which part belongs to someone else, which rule you think you broke, who taught you that rule, whether you still consider it valid, whether there is anything to repair and, if you already repaired it, why you are still punishing yourself. In writing, and without rushing.

Two things can come out of it:

  • There is nothing to repair. The rule wasn’t yours, the harm never happened, or the part that was yours is already done. Then that was guilt without responsibility, and what remains is letting go; that is what level 7 is about.
  • There is something to repair. Then repair it this week. Concrete and proportionate: a call, an apology without embellishment (“I went too far, I’m sorry, it wasn’t fair”), returning what you owed, redoing what was left badly done. Proportionate means a harsh reply is repaired with an apology, not with three weeks of service. Over-repairing is not generosity; it is self-punishment with better packaging.

Notice how the guilt feels after you repair. If it eases, it was functional and it did its job. If it stays exactly the same after you have done what was yours to do, it is no longer talking about that mistake: it is talking about something else, and that is the work of the next level.

The question to take with you

This level’s question

Is there something to repair, or am I simply punishing myself?

When to move to the next level

When you have made at least one concrete, proportionate repair, and have noticed the difference between the guilt that leaves after repairing and the guilt that stays. And when, faced with a new guilt, your first question is “what can I do?” rather than “what kind of person am I?”. Mark the level when it feels that way — not when you finish reading.
